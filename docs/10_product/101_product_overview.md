# Product Overview

## Purpose

The project is intended to turn a flat literary and audiovisual pattern list into
a controlled coding instrument. The instrument has three connected uses:

1. a coding manual with inclusion and exclusion criteria;
2. a versioned annotation record structure;
3. a pilot protocol that can test whether independent annotators apply the
   definitions consistently.

The current source vocabulary is a proposal. Its value lies in separating label
types, giving literature and video indicators, pairing inclusions with
exclusions, requiring evidence, and stating an interpretation boundary. Those
design choices need empirical testing before the taxonomy can support comparative
or computational claims.

## Intended benefits

- Separate broad pattern kinds from pattern labels, reducing undifferentiated
  tagging.
- Accommodate literary and audiovisual material without assuming equivalent
  cues.
- Make near-misses explicit through exclusion criteria.
- Require locators and evidence rather than relying on pattern names.
- Support temporal annotation through linked setup, anticipation, disruption,
  reveal, and consequence events.
- Retain provenance from the original workbook.
- Provide a structure for revision, disagreement, and later validation.

## Intended outputs

- A normalised, validated version of the current pattern vocabulary.
- A JSON or relational annotation contract with enforceable evidence rules.
- A documented text and video pilot.
- Agreement observations and adjudicated boundary cases.
- Status records based on observed evidence.
- A usable teaching and creative-review workbook once the pattern definitions
  are sufficiently stable.

## Users

- Literary and media-studies researchers.
- Computational-humanities and annotation teams.
- Students learning close reading, film analysis, or narrative construction.
- Writers, editors, and story-development teams using the patterns as prompts
  and review criteria.

These are intended user groups. No user research or adoption evidence is present
yet.
